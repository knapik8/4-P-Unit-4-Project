> The Arab-Israeli War

Preceding these wars was the Arab-Israeli War of 1948. On the midnight of May 14th, Britain's Mandate for Palestine expired, the European nation finally pulling out of the land after some 28 years. The Mandate, along with others for different territories, was created after WWI by the League of Nations when distributing said territories from one nation to another.

On this same night, David Ben-Gurion declared Israel's independence and formal existence as a new country. He would become Israel's first Prime Minister.

An important document to note is the Balfour Declaration of 1917; a British declaration stating that Israel would eventually be established as a national homeland for Jews. This declaration inspired migration of European Jews to Israel/Palestine, as well as other push factors like religious persecution and a rise in Zionist ideology. Both the increasing Jewish and British presence in Palestine upset the Palestinian Arabs who had been living there for hundreds of years.

Another document to note was the United Nations Partiton Plan for Palestine created in 1947. It basically split the land bicamerally; one half for Arabs, the other for Jews. Jerusalem was the international point. While Jewish leaders accepted this plan, the Arab leaders did not. However, it was the solution created for when the Mandate dissolved.


So, on May 15th of 1948, a coalition of Arab nations, that being Egypt, Jordan, and Syria, attempted to attack the newly founded nation of Israel. They invaded Palestine (the orange areas on the map above), took control, and started attacking Israeli settlements/forces. Something else to note; a few months before the Mandate dissolved, Zionist Israeli forces, called the Haganah, annexed depopulated towns and villages in Mandatory Palestine in their Plan Dalet, the purpose of the plan being to acquire more territory for their future Jewish state.

The Arab nations, though it was predicated to be an easy victory, lost. They lacked strength, cooperation, and unity. Their forces were weak compared to Israel's, who had acquired a ton of artillery post-WWII. They'd also received a great amount of military and economic support from Western nations.
